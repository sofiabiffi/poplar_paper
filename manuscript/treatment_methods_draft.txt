Effects of alley cropping treatment on periodic annual carbon increment (PAIC) were assessed separately during active cropping and following cessation after the final harvest in August 2004. Tree measurements were averaged within each block, treatment row, hybrid and measurement interval. Gaussian linear mixed models fitted by restricted maximum likelihood included cropping treatment, hybrid and measurement interval, together with their interactions, and block as an additive fixed effect. Interval identified the period between consecutive visits (for example, 1997–1998 or 1998–2001) and was treated as a categorical factor to account for shared temporal variation; PAIC was already annualised for elapsed time. Random intercepts for treatment row, row by hybrid and row by interval accounted for persistent spatial differences and repeated observations within the experimental units. Block was retained to respect the experimental design irrespective of its statistical significance. Treatment means and pairwise comparisons were averaged equally across hybrids. Interval-specific comparisons were obtained from the fitted models using Satterthwaite degrees of freedom, with Holm adjustment across all three treatment pairs and all observed intervals within each phase. The 2002–2005 measurement interval, spanning the cessation boundary, was excluded from the primary analyses and included with active cropping in a sensitivity analysis.
