## Historical exploration below superseded for the current paper account

Authorised 20 October 2026. Fit hybrid summaries jointly with a Gamma(log) model, hybrid-specific quadratic age trajectories (fixed 15-year centre and 10-year scaling), and a random measurement-year intercept. The random intercept accounts for shared annual conditions; four hybrids are not four independent weather replicates. Quadratic age is a provisional two-degree-of-freedom trajectory, not an established growth curve. Age, calendar time and cultivation history cannot be separately identified by this adjustment alone.

Main comparison: age baseline versus baseline plus shared April-September mean air temperature and rainfall total (per 100 mm). For numerical conditioning, temperature is centred at 13 degrees C, rainfall at 400 mm, and winter temperature at 5 degrees C; these fixed centres do not use held-out data. Use the same common complete positive-response years. Leave out all hybrids from one measurement year; refit each model and integrate over the unseen year's normally distributed random intercept for held-out mean predictions. There is no full-data fitted year effect in validation predictions. This is interpolation among observed years, not prospective forecasting; residual temporal dependence and temporally blocked validation remain follow-up items.

If the shared-weather model improves both held-out RMSE and mean Gamma deviance, inspect hybrid-specific weather slopes and addition of preceding-winter mean temperature as exploratory extensions. This gating is not independent/nested assessment of the selected winner. No claim of validated model selection or confirmatory p-values is intended. Present all assessed candidates.

Effect curves hold age at 15 years and the other weather variable at its observed mean; marginalise over year effects. Approximate 95% intervals use fixed-effect covariance, conditional on estimated variance components and model specification. They are intervals for the mean, not prediction intervals, and do not include selection uncertainty. Temperature/rainfall support and their correlation should be reviewed before interpreting one-variable curves at extremes.

## Measurement intervals: confirmed convention and manuscript wording

PAIC is annualised by dividing the carbon increment by elapsed years between successive samplings. This accounts for interval duration but does not identify the distribution of growth among years within an interval. Retain weather from growth year t - 1 for a measurement in year t, including multi-year increments, as confirmed by the user. No interval averaging or exclusion of multi-year increments is introduced here.

Methods draft: "Periodic annual carbon increment was calculated over the interval between successive measurements and annualised by interval length. Weather predictors were assigned from the growth year preceding each measurement, including measurements following multi-year intervals."

Discussion draft: "For multi-year sampling intervals, annualised PAIC represents average increment over the interval, whereas weather predictors describe only its final growth year. The timing of growth within unsampled years is unknown; weather associations therefore cannot resolve year-specific growth responses within these intervals."

Original PAIC-scale extension (analysis/13_absolute_weather_paic.R): same June-August rainfall and temperature, no additional screening. Gamma(log) original positive tree-median PAIC with hybrid-specific intercept, quadratic age and weather slopes. Absolute contrasts compare mean temperature +/-0.5 degree C or mean rain +/-50 mm, averaging over the same observed age distribution. Temperature differences (Mg C ha^-1 yr^-1): Beaupre 0.444, Gibecq 0.389, Robusta 0.532, Trichobel 0.582. Percentage temperature differences: 40.5%, 47.6%, 53.6%, 32.2%, respectively. A larger absolute response can arise from a larger baseline increment. All direct pairwise pointwise block-bootstrap intervals include zero; no firm hybrid ranking. The bootstrap has rare very large absolute contrasts from poor age-range representation, exported in a tail audit; no replicates removed. Percentile intervals are exploratory, with age extrapolation, dependence and screening uncertainty still material. Linear age sensitivity preserves the broad distinction between absolute and percentage effects. This is explanatory association, not forecasting or heat-tolerance evidence.

Hybrid-specific summer associations (analysis/12_hybrid_weather_departures.R): fully interacting linear model of standardised log PAIC departures after hybrid-specific quadratic trends. Temperature slopes per degree C: Beaupre 0.557, Gibecq 0.538, Robusta 0.686, Trichobel 0.546. Rainfall slopes per 100 mm: 0.788, 0.683, 0.623, 0.608. In-sample R2 spans 0.23-0.31 of each hybrid's standardised departures. All 12 direct slope-difference simultaneous approximate bootstrap intervals include zero; this does not prove equal responses. Paired circular blocks of three observed visits preserve some temporal dependence while refitting trends/scales. Consecutive-calendar-year residual correlations remain 0.59, 0.68, 0.59 and 0.30, respectively. Block length is provisional, prior predictor screening still applies, and the model cannot attribute longer-term Trichobel divergence to warming after that divergence has been absorbed in its age trend. Linear-trend, unstandardised-log-slope and year-omission sensitivities exported; no stronger Trichobel temperature association emerges from the first two sensitivities.

Shared-departure model follow-up (analysis/11_weather_departure_models.R): 26 years, fold-wise training-only hybrid detrending and residual standardisation. Leave-one-year-out RMSE: no weather 1.198, rainfall 1.158, temperature 1.166, both 1.032 (13.9% reduction). Linear detrending also gives a combined-model improvement (15.8%). Prior full-data screening means this is exploratory, not independent validation of candidate selection. Omitting 1994 from scoring only reduces the combined advantage to about 6%, highlighting influence. Contiguous block error is much higher (quadratic baseline 3.001, both 2.459); the earliest block's age extrapolation produces a shared held-out departure near -9 training SDs. Do not use this as evidence of reliable long-range forecasting. The shared-departure scale is not raw PAIC and these RMSEs cannot be directly compared with the earlier Gamma models. Full-data curves condition on detrending. Existing t - 1 alignment and all earlier caveats remain in force.

Exploratory weather-departure screen (analysis/10_weather_departure_screen.R): nine predefined candidates on 24 common complete-coverage years. ## First results, 20 October 2026

26 common measurement years, 104 hybrid summaries. Whole-year held-out RMSE: baseline 1.6956, weather 1.7025 Mg C ha^-1 yr^-1; mean Gamma deviance: baseline 0.60735, weather 0.61216. Shared temperature and rainfall did not improve either metric. Hybrid-specific weather slopes and winter-temperature extensions were therefore not fitted. Both shared weather coefficient 95% Wald intervals include zero. This does not demonstrate absence of weather effects, and does not settle alternative exposure windows or age specifications.

After numerical conditioning and tighter optimisation, all 54 full/fold fits have no recorded convergence messages and no singular random-effect fits. Held-out predictions are finite and positive with exactly one prediction per model/year/hybrid. Full model residuals are exported to results/checks/weather_temperature_rainfall_residuals.csv; deeper residual temporal diagnostics remain open.
